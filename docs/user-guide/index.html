Field Guide & User Manual

How to use BlueGreen Guide

A practical guide to the current field-test build: map-first discovery, Water and Land wayfinding, mobile navigation, search, filters, collections, place details, Launch Suitability guidance, and verification notes.

Current field-test build

The current v1.2 field-test runtime resolves 89 active places. Most are still part of the original water/paddle discovery use case, with a deliberately small green/mixed pilot added to test the broader BlueGreen model. Mixed destinations intentionally participate in both Water and Land discovery.

Desktop and tablet

On larger screens, the map remains the central discovery surface while search, filters, collections, results, and place details sit alongside it. Use the map and list together: narrow the results, inspect markers, and open details without treating any single label as a guarantee.

Water, Land, and mixed places

Water is for water-centered places and activities. Land is for land-centered places and activities. Mixed is not a new brand color; it describes places with meaningful blue-space and green-space characteristics. Mixed places can appear in either relevant discovery filter.

Color is not the only meaning: BlueGreen Guide also uses labels, icon shapes, and place/activity names so the interface remains understandable without relying on color alone.

Curated collections

Collections are editorial shortlists based on explicit place IDs. Selecting one filters the current results. Collections can be combined with search and applicable filters.

Explore the map

Pan and zoom, select markers, fit all places when needed, or filter around the visible map area. Blue markers communicate water semantics and green markers communicate land semantics. Mixed destinations use the appropriate blue/green semantics rather than a separate arbitrary color.

Search

Search uses place names, aliases, regions, water bodies, taxonomy, activities, amenities, tags, descriptions, and paddle-suitability values where relevant. The same search should help whether you know a place name or only know the kind of experience you want.

Filters

Water/Land discovery is the broad environment-level filter. Paddle-relevant places also retain region, skill, water-activity, and maximum-difficulty filters. These can be combined with search and curated collections.

Why some fields are paddle-specific: BlueGreen Guide began with paddleboarding and kayaking. The green-space pilot is intentionally small, so land places are not forced to carry paddle-only fields simply to fit the older model.

Place cards

Place cards summarize the most useful information available for a record. Paddle-relevant cards may prioritize SUP Suitability, Difficulty, Wind Sensitivity, Typical Use, Staging Space, activities, amenities, and verification status. Green-space cards focus on the place and land-relevant information available in the field-test record.

The legacy numeric Popularity field may still exist internally during migration, but it is not the preferred user-facing crowd signal. Typical Use and Crowd Sensitivity communicate that planning context more directly.

Place details

Open a place detail view for the fuller description, relevant planning fields, image information, source links, environment context, verification notes, and Launch Suitability fields where the place is paddle-relevant.

Launch Suitability Profile

The Launch Suitability Profile is a paddle-specific planning layer. It helps compare paddle-relevant places but is not a live-conditions system or safety score.

SUP Suitability

Overall recreational stand-up paddleboarding fit: Excellent, Good, Fair, or Challenging.

Wind Sensitivity

How strongly increasing wind can degrade the paddling experience. It is not current wind data.

Typical Use

A general low-to-very-high use signal. It is not a live visitor count.

Crowd Sensitivity

How much crowding can interfere with launching, staging, resting, or paddling.

Staging Space

Practical room for unloading, rigging, launching, exiting, and temporarily placing paddle equipment.

Assessment Confidence

Confidence in the curated suitability assessment. It is separate from factual place verification.

Difficulty, Skill Level, and Best Time remain separate planning signals. Always compare them with current conditions and your own experience, equipment, and comfort level.

Verification and sources

Official sourceNeeds verificationConditions vary

An official source link does not automatically verify every field. Access, legal activity areas, parking, fees, facilities, closures, water quality, fire restrictions, trail conditions, weather, wind, tides, and hazards may require separate current checks.

Why do so many places say “Needs verification”?

BlueGreen Guide intentionally favors transparency over assumptions. A managing agency may confirm that a park, launch, lake, trail system, or facility exists without confirming every operational detail in the record. The app keeps material uncertainty visible instead of converting partial evidence into a fully verified claim.

Verification status describes confidence in available place facts. It does not mean a destination is good, bad, safe, or suitable for every person.

Photography

Existing paddle records may use credited representative photography. New green/mixed field-test photography still needs verification in some cases. Treat image labels and credits literally: a representative image is not proof that the photo shows the exact access point or current conditions.

Map coordinates

Markers aim to represent practical visitor, launch, shoreline-access, or trail-access locations where that can be reasonably supported. When an exact GPS point is inferred rather than published by an official source, it should be documented as representative rather than presented as an official waypoint.

Before you go

Confirm access

Check current official access, parking, fees, hours, closures, permits, and local rules.

Check conditions

Review the current conditions relevant to your activity—weather, wind, tides, water quality, trail status, fire restrictions, or other advisories.

Use your own judgment

Consider experience, equipment, companions, mobility needs, and comfort level. BlueGreen Guide planning labels do not replace personal judgment or official guidance.
